ALINA: Advanced Line Identification and Notation Algorithm

CVPR 2024

Mohammed Abdul Hafeez Khan1, Parth Ganeriwala1, Siddhartha Bhattacharyya1, Natasha Neogi2, Raja Muthalagu3

1Florida Institute of Technology    2NASA Langley Research Center    3BITS Pilani Dubai Campus

Paper · Code · Citation

Supervised learning needs labels, and for taxiway line markings that usually means hand-labeling or crowd-sourcing thousands of video frames. That's slow, expensive, and error-prone, especially across the different camera angles and weather conditions a real taxiway dataset covers.

ALINA needs one region of interest, drawn once on the first frame of a video. It warps that region into a top-down view, isolates line marking pixels by color, and traces the connected pixels with a custom algorithm, CIRCLEDAT. The same region is then reused automatically across every remaining frame, no further input needed.

Highlights

No manual pixel labeling

Replaces slow, costly, crowd-sourced annotation with one region of interest per video.

Perspective- and weather-agnostic

Works across different camera angles and both sunny and cloudy conditions.

98.45%

Detection rate against a hand-verified ground truth set.

60,249 frames

Labeled from the AssistTaxi dataset using this approach.

Method

  1. Draw a trapezoidal region of interest on the first frame.
  2. Warp the region into a top-down view.
  3. Normalize and threshold the color channels to isolate candidate line marking pixels.
  4. Scan a histogram of the mask to locate the strongest column of pixels.
  5. Trace the connected line marking pixels with CIRCLEDAT.
  6. Unwarp the result and mark the pixels on the original frame.

The same region of interest is reused for every later frame in the video. No model training involved.
